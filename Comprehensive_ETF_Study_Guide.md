## 1. ETF Primary Market & Creation/Redemption Mechanism

### Navigation and Structure
The ETF primary market operates through a hierarchical structure:

**Flow Diagram Components:**
- **Top Level**: Fund/ETF Sponsor creates the product
- **Middle Level**: Authorized Participants (APs) interact with the fund
- **Bottom Level**: Securities basket is exchanged

### Creation Process
1. AP assembles a basket of underlying securities
2. AP delivers securities to the ETF sponsor
3. ETF sponsor creates new ETF shares (creation units, typically 50,000 shares)
4. AP receives ETF shares in return
5. AP can sell these shares on the secondary market

### Redemption Process
1. AP accumulates ETF shares from the market
2. AP delivers creation unit to ETF sponsor
3. ETF sponsor redeems shares
4. AP receives basket of underlying securities
5. AP can sell securities in the market

### Key Points
- This mechanism is what keeps ETF market price close to NAV
- Only occurs in large blocks (creation units)
- Typically done in-kind (securities for shares), not cash
- Creates tax efficiency advantages

## 2. ETF Basket Structure

### What is an ETF Basket?
An ETF basket represents the precise collection of securities that an Authorized Participant must deliver to create new ETF shares, or will receive when redeeming ETF shares.

### Basket Characteristics
- **Composition**: Mirrors the ETF's holdings proportionally
- **Transparency**: Published daily (usually)
- **Flexibility**: Can be adjusted by fund manager
- **In-Kind Transactions**: Securities are not sold, just transferred

### Why Baskets Matter
1. **Tax Efficiency**: In-kind transfers don't trigger capital gains
2. **Price Alignment**: Arbitrage keeps market price near NAV
3. **Liquidity**: Basket liquidity determines ETF liquidity
4. **Cost Management**: Reduces transaction costs

### Basket vs Geography
Different geographic holdings may have different:
- Trading hours
- Liquidity profiles
- Settlement periods
- Regulatory requirements

## 3. Premium and Discount Dynamics

### Definitions
- **Premium**: ETF trades ABOVE its NAV (Market Price > NAV)
- **Discount**: ETF trades BELOW its NAV (Market Price < NAV)
- **Fair Value**: When Market Price ≈ NAV

### Why Premiums/Discounts Occur

**Premiums happen when:**
- High demand for ETF shares
- Underlying securities are difficult to obtain
- Market expects NAV to increase
- Creation process is temporarily constrained

**Discounts happen when:**
- High selling pressure on ETF
- Underlying securities are easily available
- Market expects NAV to decrease
- Redemption demand is high

### Arbitrage Mechanism
1. **When Premium exists**:
   - APs buy underlying securities
   - Create new ETF shares
   - Sell ETF shares at premium
   - Profit = Premium amount
   - This increases supply, reducing premium

2. **When Discount exists**:
   - APs buy ETF shares at discount
   - Redeem for underlying securities
   - Sell securities at fair value
   - Profit = Discount amount
   - This decreases supply, reducing discount

### Normal Range
- Most ETFs trade within 0.25% of NAV
- Wider spreads for:
  - International ETFs
  - Less liquid underlying securities
  - During market stress

## 4. ETF Pricing: IV vs Market Price vs NAV

### Three Key Prices to Understand

**1. Net Asset Value (NAV)**
- Calculated once per day at market close
- Sum of all holdings' values ÷ shares outstanding
- Official "book value" of the ETF

**2. Intraday Value (IV) / Indicative Optimized Portfolio Value (IOPV)**
- Calculated every 15 seconds during trading day
- Real-time estimate of what NAV would be
- Based on current prices of underlying securities

**3. Market Price**
- The actual trading price on the exchange
- Determined by supply and demand
- Updates continuously during trading hours

### Relationships Between Prices
From the charts in the materials:
- **IV tracks underlying securities in real-time**
- **Market Price follows IV closely**
- **NAV is calculated end-of-day**
- All three should converge at market close

### Why They Differ
- **Time zones**: International holdings trade at different times
- **Trading hours**: ETF may trade when underlying markets are closed
- **Supply/Demand**: Short-term imbalances
- **Stale pricing**: Some securities haven't traded recently

## 5. ETFs During Market Dislocations

### What are Market Dislocations?
Periods of extreme market stress where normal relationships break down:
- Flash crashes
- Liquidity crises
- Market circuit breakers
- Major news events

### ETF Behavior During Dislocations

**Key Observations from Charts:**
1. **Wider spreads**: Premium/discount ranges increase significantly
2. **Greater volatility**: Price swings become more dramatic
3. **Temporary divergence**: Market price may deviate more from NAV
4. **Recovery**: Prices typically realign after stress passes

### Why This Happens
- APs become more risk-averse
- Underlying securities become harder to price
- Bid-ask spreads widen
- Creation/redemption may slow down
- Market makers reduce inventory

### Important Point
- **Price discovery function**: ETFs may actually help price underlying securities during stress
- **Not a breakdown**: This is the market working, not failing
- **Temporary**: Usually resolves within hours or days

## 7. Momentum and Trend-Following

### Quantifying Momentum: Adding Dimension to Trend-Following

### What is Momentum?
The tendency for securities that have performed well (or poorly) recently to continue that performance.

### Types of Momentum

**1. Price Momentum (12-1 Month)**
- Look at returns over past 12 months, skipping most recent month
- Skip recent month to avoid reversal effects
- Most common momentum measure

**2. Short-term Momentum (1-3 Month)**
- More recent performance
- Higher turnover
- More reactive

**3. Long-term Momentum (3-5 Year)**
- Longer trends
- Lower turnover
- More stable

### Dimensional Approach to Momentum
From the materials, key considerations include:

**Timing and Measurement:**
- Not just picking winners
- Consider trading costs
- Avoid overcrowding in popular trades
- Integrate with other factors

**Risk Management:**
- Momentum can reverse quickly
- Need discipline in implementation
- Consider transaction costs
- Avoid excessive turnover

- Different market regimes

## 8. Portfolio Weighting Strategies

### Overweighting vs Underweighting

### Traditional Approaches

**1. Market-Cap Weighting**
- Weight by company size
- Most common in index funds
- Self-balancing
- Can lead to concentration

**2. Equal Weighting**
- Each position same size
- Requires regular rebalancing
- Smaller cap bias
- Higher turnover

**3. Factor Weighting**
- Weight by factor exposure
- Value, size, profitability, etc.
- Research-driven
- Active management

### "Overweighting Less" Concept
From the materials, this refers to:

**Moderate Tilts vs Extreme Positions**
- Don't need extreme positions to capture premiums
- Balance factor exposure with diversification
- Reduce concentration risk
- Manage trading costs

**Practical Implementation:**
- Small but persistent tilts
- Diversify across many securities
- Avoid betting everything on one factor
- Long-term discipline

### Portfolio Construction Principles
1. **Diversification**: Spread risk across many holdings
2. **Cost Management**: Don't overtrade
3. **Factor Balance**: Multiple sources of expected return
4. **Risk Control**: Avoid excessive concentration

## 10. Cash-in-Kind Component

### Understanding Cash-in-Kind Transactions

From the Dimensional Fund Advisors materials:

### What is Cash-in-Kind?

**In-Kind Transaction:**
- AP delivers securities, receives ETF shares (or vice versa)
- NO cash changes hands
- Most common method

**Cash Component:**
- Sometimes small cash portion included
- Balances out odd lots
- Handles difficult-to-transfer securities
- Adjusts for corporate actions

### When Cash is Used

**Reasons for Cash Component:**
1. **Fractional shares**: Can't deliver partial shares
2. **Foreign securities**: Settlement issues
3. **Restricted securities**: Transfer limitations
4. **Dividend timing**: Recent distributions
5. **Rebalancing needs**: Minor portfolio adjustments

### AP Transaction Process

**Creation with Cash Component:**
1. AP delivers 95-99% in securities
2. Remaining 1-5% in cash
3. Fund sponsor completes the basket
4. AP receives ETF shares

**Redemption with Cash Component:**
1. AP delivers ETF shares
2. Receives 95-99% in securities
3. Receives 1-5% in cash
4. AP has nearly complete basket

### Tax Implications

**In-Kind (Preferred):**
- No capital gains realized
- Tax-efficient for fund
- Securities transferred at cost basis

**Cash Component:**
- May require security sales
- Potential capital gains
- Less tax-efficient
- Usually minimal impact

### Why It Matters

**Occasionally, ETFs may allow or require cash for the following reasons:**
- Basket is difficult to obtain (e.g., emerging markets, in-kind transactions are not permissible in the local market)
- Securities in the basket are illiquid or there are cost advantages to having the AP offset any transaction fees/slippage to offset any transaction cost to the ETF of buying or selling those securities on its own
- AP's may also incur transaction fees to offset any transaction cost to the ETF

## 11. ETFs vs Mutual Funds

### Separating Myth from Reality: Actively Managed Funds vs ETFs

From the comparison charts in the materials:

### Structural Differences

| Feature | ETFs | Mutual Funds |
|---------|------|--------------|
| **Trading** | Intraday, like stocks | End of day only |
| **Pricing** | Market price (continuous) | NAV (once daily) |
| **Creation** | In-kind (usually) | Cash |
| **Tax Efficiency** | Generally higher | Generally lower |
| **Minimum Investment** | One share | Often $1,000-$3,000 |
| **Expense Ratios** | Often lower | Often higher |

### Performance Comparison

The charts show:
- Price movements over time
- NAV comparisons
- Total return metrics

**Key Finding:** When comparing similar strategies:
- Returns should be similar (before costs)
- ETF structure may provide cost advantages
- Tax efficiency favors ETFs for taxable accounts
- Mutual funds may be better for:
  - Automatic investment plans
  - Dollar-cost averaging programs
  - Retirement accounts (tax doesn't matter)

### Tax Efficiency Advantage

**Why ETFs Are More Tax Efficient:**
1. In-kind creation/redemption
2. Can dispose of low-basis shares
3. Investors control when they sell
4. No forced distributions from others' redemptions

**Mutual Fund Challenge:**
- Redemptions require selling securities
- All shareholders share capital gains
- Less control over tax timing

### When to Choose Each

**Choose ETF when:**
- Taxable account
- Want intraday trading
- Prefer lower costs
- Like transparency

**Choose Mutual Fund when:**
- Retirement account
- Want automatic investments
- Prefer simplicity
- Don't need intraday liquidity

## 12. ETF Liquidity Analysis

### Appendix: Cumulative ETF Liquidity

From the detailed data tables and formulas:

### Understanding ETF Liquidity

**Common Misconception:**
"Trading volume = liquidity"

**Reality:**
ETF liquidity comes from UNDERLYING SECURITIES, not just ETF trading volume

### Liquidity Formula

The materials present the concept:

**True Liquidity = f(Underlying Security Liquidity, Creation/Redemption Capacity, Market Maker Activity)**

Where:
- **Underlying Security Liquidity**: Can APs easily obtain/sell the basket?
- **Creation/Redemption Capacity**: How quickly can new shares be created/redeemed?
- **Market Maker Activity**: How many firms provide quotes?

### Cumulative Liquidity Metrics

From the extensive data table:

**Measures Include:**
1. **Average Daily Volume (ADV)** of ETF
2. **Average Daily Volume** of underlying securities
3. **Shares Outstanding**
4. **Assets Under Management**
5. **Average Spread**
6. **Creation/Redemption Activity**

### Liquidity Tiers

**Tier 1: Highly Liquid**
- Tight spreads (<0.05%)
- Deep markets
- Multiple market makers
- Easy to trade in size

**Tier 2: Moderately Liquid**
- Moderate spreads (0.05-0.25%)
- Good market depth
- Some size capacity
- May need to work larger orders

**Tier 3: Less Liquid**
- Wider spreads (>0.25%)
- Thinner markets
- Limited size capacity
- Require careful trading

### Trading Best Practices

**For Large Orders:**
1. Don't rely solely on displayed volume
2. Contact market makers
3. Work the order over time
4. Consider underlying liquidity
5. Use limit orders

**Red Flags:**
- Wide spreads despite liquid underlying
- Low creation/redemption activity
- Few market makers
- Stale pricing

## Key Formulas and Calculations

### 1. NAV Calculation
```
NAV = (Total Assets - Total Liabilities) / Shares Outstanding
```

### 2. Premium/Discount Calculation
```
Premium/Discount % = ((Market Price - NAV) / NAV) × 100
```

**Example:**
- NAV = $50.00
- Market Price = $50.25
- Premium = ((50.25 - 50.00) / 50.00) × 100 = 0.50%

### 3. Total Return
```
Total Return = (Ending Price - Beginning Price + Distributions) / Beginning Price
```

### 4. Expense Ratio Impact
```
Net Return = Gross Return - Expense Ratio
```

**Example over 10 years:**
- Gross Return: 8% annually
- Expense Ratio: 0.50%
- Net Return: 7.50% annually
- Difference compounds significantly over time

## Quick Reference Guide

### ETF Trading Cheat Sheet

**When buying an ETF:**
1. Check the spread
2. Use limit orders for less liquid ETFs
3. Avoid market orders at open/close
4. Consider underlying market hours

**Red flags to watch for:**
- Spread > 0.50% for liquid holdings
- Large premium/discount (>1%)
- Very low volume AND illiquid underlying
- Stale NAV pricing

### Factor Investing Quick Guide

**Value**: Cheap > Expensive (Price-to-Book)
**Size**: Small > Large (Market Cap)
**Profitability**: Profitable > Unprofitable (ROE, margins)
**Momentum**: Recent Winners > Recent Losers (12-1 month return)

**Combine factors for better diversification!**
